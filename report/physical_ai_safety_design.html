Defense in Depth for Physical AI

A design for AI systems that understand the physics, perform engineering tasks correctly, and manage work at scale, built from what nuclear, medical and space safety learned the hard way

Tuan Anh Dinh. ProximaB design note, v2, 8 November 2026. Deep-research draft for review.

v2 integrates three earlier ProximaB documents: The Engineering Brain (research report, September 2026) with its six-layer stack; the Defense-in-Depth Architecture note that maps those layers to ABB Atom, FDA V&V 40 and space FDIR practice; and PRD v2.0 Rev B. The failure modes now carry the FM identifiers from that note, the barriers are placed in the six layers, and the event-tree arithmetic for the stack is written out with the pilot's measured values.

Summary

Nuclear power, medical devices and crewed spaceflight each became safe through the same sequence: define the failure modes, place independent barriers against them, and grade the proof of each barrier by the consequence of its failure. None of them trusts a single clever component. All of them keep a last line of defense that does not share the failure modes of the clever part, and all of them feed every incident back into the design. AI safety for physical engineering should start from that sequence rather than from the AI.

This note does four things.

  1. It states the failure-mode taxonomy for AI engineering agents under the three requirements you set: the AI must understand the physics and chemistry (FM-1.x), it must perform the task with the right function and the right method (FM-2.x), and it must be able to manage work at the scale of a plant, a drug program or a mission (FM-3.x). Each mode has a known analogue in a regulated industry, and the pilot we ran in Releases 0 and 0.1 has measured several of them.
  2. It lays out a five-level defense-in-depth design for AI engineering agents, modelled on the IAEA levels and on the diversity-and-defense-in-depth rules for digital instrumentation and control, with a graded-oversight rule taken from how the FDA and ASME decide how much credibility a computational model must earn before a decision rests on it.
  3. It places every barrier in the six-layer Engineering Brain stack (model, harness, skills, solvers and system model, evidence ledger, benchmarks), so the design is also a build plan for the two ProximaB codebases, and it writes the event tree for the stack so that residual risk can be computed from measured barrier failure rates rather than asserted.
  4. It turns the design into a research program: what to measure, in what order, with the pilot results as the first data points, and it lists the claims in the existing ProximaB documents that must be verified before they are cited.

The single most useful transfer from nuclear is the idea of common-cause failure. A monitor that shares the agent's model family, training data, prompt or information is not an independent barrier, any more than three pressure transmitters on one impulse line are three channels. In the pilot, Haiku as an agent and Haiku as a monitor reached the same wrong conclusion on the same task for the same reason. That is a beta factor, and it has to be assessed, measured and designed against. The second most useful transfer is the one reactor safety design rests on: you cannot prove a complex system will never fail, so you separate it into divisions that cannot take each other down, and you put a passive barrier between the worst failure and the public.

0. The stack the barriers live in

The Engineering Brain describes an AI engineering system as six layers: the foundation model; the harness that runs the agent loop; the skills that load discipline and regulatory knowledge; the tools, solvers and shared system model that do the physics; the evidence ledger that records what a regulator will ask for; and the benchmarks that score the whole configuration. Its finding is that the harness layer has converged on two generic winners, that every measured gain has come from skills and typed tools, and that the evidence layer is mandated by regulators and built by almost no one.

Read as a safety architecture, each layer is where a particular class of barrier lives. The table reconciles the IAEA levels used in this note (plus a Level 0 for qualification before use) with the layer mapping in the earlier architecture note, and says where each barrier exists today across the two codebases: the Safety Gates repository (eng-safety-pilot, this pilot) and the local harness (ProximaB/proximab_harness, with its solvers, verifiers, evidence ledger and certifier).

1. What the regulated industries learned

1.1 Nuclear: levels, independence, separation, and the digital common-cause problem

The five levels of defense in depth were set out in INSAG-3 in 1988 and refined in INSAG-10; the IAEA design requirements (SSR-2/1, Rev. 1) keep that framework and require that the levels be independent of one another as far as practicable, and that the means for mitigating severe accidents be largely independent of the systems used for more frequent events. The levels are: prevention of abnormal operation; detection and control of abnormal operation; control of accidents within the design basis; control of severe conditions, including preventing progression and mitigating consequences; and mitigation of off-site consequences.

The ABB Atom reactors designed in Västerås are an example, and the earlier architecture note records the specifics: the BWR 75 and BWR 90 designs with four physically separated safety divisions, so that a fire or a flood in one quadrant cannot take the others down; and the severe-accident mitigation that assumes the active systems have all failed, including filtered containment venting (FILTRA at Barsebäck, the multi-venturi scrubber systems) and the lower-drywell core catcher. The axiom behind it, in the note's words: you cannot prove that a complex system will never fail, so you exhaustively define its failure modes, enforce physical and functional separation so failures cannot cascade, eliminate common-cause failures through diversity, and place deterministic, passive barriers between any component failure and a catastrophic outcome. Designing that system meant treating software and hardware reliability together, and it started by naming the failure modes. That is the step most AI safety work skips.

Three further nuclear lessons bear directly on AI. First, software introduced a new kind of failure that hardware redundancy does not address. Identical software in redundant channels fails identically, so the US NRC's Branch Technical Position 7-19 (Revision 9, 2024) requires applicants to assess defense in depth and diversity against common-cause failure from latent design defects in digital safety systems, and NUREG/CR-6303 gives the method: six attributes of diversity (design, equipment, functional, human, signal, software), with a diverse or manual backup where the assessment finds a vulnerability. Second, analysis methods are qualified before they are trusted. NRC Regulatory Guide 1.203 (the evaluation model development and assessment process) begins with a Phenomena Identification and Ranking Table: before anyone writes an input deck, the engineers name the phenomena that govern the transient and rank their importance, and the code is then shown to be adequate for exactly those phenomena. Third, the industry learned after Three Mile Island that the barriers decay unless every event is reported, analysed and fed back: the operating-experience programs (INPO's SEE-IN, now INPO 10-006) and the plant corrective-action programs that the NRC inspects are as much a part of defense in depth as the containment building. Fukushima added the reminder that a common cause can lie outside the design basis: one tsunami defeated every emergency diesel generator at once.

1.2 Medical: risk-graded evidence and change control

ISO 14971 makes risk management the spine of device development, and IEC 62304 ties software rigor to a safety class. The lesson that makes this concrete is the Therac-25: its predecessors had hardware interlocks against overdose, the Therac-25 relied on software for the same protection, a race condition defeated it, and patients died. The FDA's January 2025 draft guidance on AI-enabled device software functions carries the same thinking into AI: a marketing submission must describe risk assessment, data management, model development, validation, and performance monitoring across the whole lifecycle, including the risk that performance degrades after deployment; and the predetermined change control plan guidance (final, December 2024) lets a sponsor pre-specify how a model may change and how each change will be verified, instead of re-submitting.

The most transferable medical rule is ASME V&V 40 (2018), which the FDA adopted in its guidance on the credibility of computational models. Model risk is the product of model influence (how much of the decision rests on the model rather than other evidence) and decision consequence (how bad a wrong decision is). That risk sets the credibility level the model must earn, through verification, validation and applicability evidence. If the evidence falls short, the options are to gather more, improve the model, or reduce its influence on the decision. This is exactly the rule an organization needs for deciding how much oversight an AI agent's decision requires. V&V 40 also separates code verification from calculation verification: a single run on one mesh or one step size is never credible evidence, because the discretization error has not been bounded. The practical tool is Richardson extrapolation across two or more resolutions, summarized as a grid convergence index (ASME V&V 20, NASA-STD-7009).

1.3 Space and aviation: fault tolerance, the validated envelope, partitioning, FDIR and learning assurance

NASA's human-rating requirements (NPR 8705.2) demand that no two failures, no two human errors, and no combination of one of each cause loss of crew, and that the system tolerate an inadvertent operator action in the presence of a single failure. Emergency systems do not count toward that tolerance. Flight rules pre-decide responses to foreseen conditions so that the decision under pressure is a lookup, not an improvisation. Spacecraft fault management is organised as fault detection, isolation and recovery (FDIR) in tiers: a component tier that catches and retries local faults within bounded budgets, a subsystem tier that isolates a persistently failing unit and switches to a redundant or dissimilar one, and a system tier that puts the vehicle in a safe mode and waits for the ground. Avionics isolate software partitions in space and time (ARINC 653), so that one misbehaving application cannot starve or corrupt another.

Two space failures are failure modes of AI agents in miniature. Ariane 5 Flight 501 (1996) reused inertial reference software from Ariane 4; a horizontal-velocity value outside the range the software had been validated for caused an operand error and the loss of the vehicle. The Mars Climate Orbiter (1999) was lost because one team's software produced thruster impulse in pound-force seconds and the other's expected newton seconds. Validated envelope and units are the two most common hazards in our task suite, and the agents' handling of them was the thing we measured.

For machine learning, EASA's concept paper for Level 1 and 2 AI applications adds a learning-assurance "W-shaped" process beside the classical V-cycle: the data, the training and the learned model each get their own verification steps, because the behaviour was learned rather than specified. ISO/IEC TR 5469 (2024) gives sector-independent guidance on AI in safety functions, ISO/PAS 8800 (2024) applies it to road vehicles alongside ISO 26262, and UL 4600 requires a safety case for autonomous products in which "safe" means an acceptable post-mitigation risk that the case must justify. The AI safety literature has arrived at the same place from the other direction: safety cases for frontier AI built on inability, control, trustworthiness or deference arguments (Clymer et al., 2024), with control as the argument available now.

1.4 Autonomous systems: STPA and runtime assurance

Two methods from autonomy complete the set. Leveson's STAMP/STPA treats safety as a control problem: accidents happen when a controller's model of the process it controls is wrong, and the hazard analysis enumerates the unsafe control actions, which come in four kinds: an action provided when it is unsafe, not provided when needed, provided in the wrong order or at the wrong time, or applied too long or stopped too soon. The method finds accidents in which every component worked as specified, which component-level FMEA cannot. The Simplex architecture, standardised for aviation as ASTM F3269 run-time assurance, wraps an untrusted high-performance controller inside a simple, verified monitor and a verified fallback: the monitor watches the state against a safety envelope and, when the complex controller would leave it, switches control to the fallback. It is how a learned controller can fly without the learned part being certified; the certification rests on the monitor and the switch.

2. The failure-mode taxonomy

The three requirements you set correspond to three families of failure modes, numbered FM-1.x, FM-2.x and FM-3.x as in the earlier architecture note, extended with the modes the pilot measured. The tables are the start of an FMEA for AI engineering agents: each row names the mode, an example or mechanism, how it would be detected, the barrier that addresses it (with its layer and level from Section 0), and the industry analogue. Rows marked with a pilot reference were measured in Releases 0 and 0.1. The STPA view in Section 2.3 adds the accidents that FMEA misses.

3. The design: five levels of defense in depth for AI engineering agents

The levels follow the IAEA structure. Each level has a purpose, barriers, the evidence that qualifies the barriers, and an independence requirement with respect to the levels beside it. Each barrier is tagged with the layer of the stack it lives in and marked built (exists in one of the two codebases), partial, or to build.

Level 0: qualification before use (layer 6, with layer 5 as the record)

Nothing enters service without evidence, and the evidence is graded by consequence.

  • Capability qualification per domain and task class (built): computed-ground-truth evaluations (the Safety Gates format), with Pass@1 and Pass^k, hazard-class breakdowns, and pressure variants. Blind human solves on every task (to do). Perturbed re-runs (the local battery's plus or minus 4 percent perturbations) are a second form of Pass^k and belong in the same report.
  • Validated envelope declared (to build): the task classes, document types, units, and consequence classes the agent is qualified for, in the way an operational design domain is declared for a vehicle or a domain of validation for a surrogate.
  • Learning assurance for learned components (to build): for any surrogate, twin or QSAR in the loop, the W-shaped evidence (data, training, learned model) plus V&V 20/40 credibility proportional to influence and consequence.
  • Configuration pinned (built): model version, harness version, tools, prompts and thresholds recorded on every result; a change control plan that pre-specifies which updates are allowed and what regression suite they must pass (to build).

Level 1: prevention in normal operation (layer 3, with layer 4's typed tools)

The agent is given tools and procedures that make the common failure modes hard to commit.

  • PIRT pre-flight declaration (to build; the local note's Barrier 1A): before any solver call the agent emits a structured declaration of the phenomena, regimes and dimensionless groups that govern the problem (Reynolds, Prandtl, Damköhler, Péclet, reduced temperature and pressure, reactivity in units of beta) and the constitutive models it will use. A deterministic rule engine checks it: an ideal-gas selection above a reduced pressure of about 0.2, or above 10 bar, is rejected in favour of a cubic or Helmholtz equation of state; a reactivity insertion above half of beta-effective requires coupled Doppler feedback and delayed-neutron groups rather than steady-state criticality. This is RG 1.203's first element applied to the agent instead of the analyst.
  • Unit type system (partial): a unit-carrying calculator that refuses dimensionally inconsistent expressions and distinguishes gauge from absolute pressure, thermal from electrical megawatts, and pcm from dollars; every check reported in the requirement's unit. Safety Gates has the safe calculator and unit-aware comparison; the typed quantity layer on tool inputs is to build.
  • Method registry and method-selection matrix (partial): the formula or procedure of record is code, named in the task contract, and the skill pack carries a matrix from problem class to qualified method, with pre- and post-conditions on each tool: stiffness ratio above about 10^3 forces an implicit integrator with a Jacobian; shielding problems require buildup factors or continuous-energy transport. This closes FM-1.7, FM-2.1 and FM-2.2.
  • Format contract (partial): every acceptance check is reported in the requirement's unit and formulation, so that a deterministic checker can compare it. The contract is published to the agent as part of the procedure (Release 0.2 item).
  • Envelope attestation (partial): any number taken from a model output or an empirical correlation must be accompanied by the validated envelope and the operating point, from the validation document, before it may be used. The pilot's four AI-in-the-loop tasks test the agent's side; the tool-side enforcement is to build.
  • Evidence trail by construction (built): the governing documents for a decision are enumerated; a submission that has not opened them is incomplete.

Level 2: detection and control of abnormal states (layer 2, with the monitors)

Independent monitors and gates review every consequential step and route doubtful ones to a human.

  • Pre- and post-condition gates on every tool call (partial): the precondition validates schema completeness, domain bounds and the absence of stale placeholders; the postcondition validates exit status, residual norm and output completeness. A failed gate returns a diagnostic to the agent for a bounded number of repair turns (Tier-1 FDIR), after which the step escalates.
  • Convergence gate (to build; the local note's Barrier 2B): for any mesh-based or time-stepped solver, the harness runs at least two resolutions and computes a grid convergence index; for Monte Carlo transport it checks the statistical uncertainty on k-effective (of order 10 pcm) and the stationarity of the fission source before tallies are scored. A run that fails the gate is flagged as FM-2.3 whatever its exit code.
  • Diversity requirement (the BTP 7-19 transfer) (built in the monitor set; the beta measurement is to do): at least one monitor must differ from the agent in model family, and at least one in information (documents rather than transcript), or be deterministic. The common-cause beta between agent and monitor is assessed and, where possible, measured by agreement on known-wrong decisions.
  • Grounded monitoring (built): monitors receive the source documents, not only the agent's transcript. Release 0.1 is the measurement: at a 2 percent false-alarm budget, grounded monitors caught 100 percent of simulated misses with the hazard document unread; the transcript-only monitor caught 63 percent.
  • Calibrated thresholds (built): each monitor's threshold is set to an audit budget on real honest traffic, and its false-alarm rate is a primary metric, because an overseer who is flagged 25 percent of the time stops reading.
  • "Cannot verify" is a flag (built): a check that the deterministic layer cannot compare goes to a human, as a plant registry would treat an unreadable surveillance record.
  • Monitor the monitors (built): suspicion calibration, quote-verification rate and agreement between monitors are tracked as operating data.

Level 3: containment of design-basis failures (layer 4, with the oversight rule)

When the agent is wrong and the monitors are uncertain, the damage is bounded by physics checks that do not depend on the agent and by how actions are allowed to execute.

  • Conservation and second-law gateways (partial; ThermoVerifier and ProcessVerifier check output tolerances, the explicit residual gateway is to build): every solver output is audited before the agent may use it. Relative closure of mass and elemental balances and of the energy balance within 10^-4; entropy generation non-negative; feedback signs as expected (reactivity coefficient negative where the design requires it). The thresholds are design choices to be justified per domain, not constants.
  • Dissimilar second channel (to build; the local note's Barrier 2C): the high-fidelity channel (agent plus solver) is cross-checked by a reduced-order or analytical channel with no model in it (point kinetics against transport; lumped mass-energy balance against the flowsheet; buildup-factor attenuation against Monte Carlo). A comparator passes the result when the two agree within a stated tolerance and the invariants hold; a discrepancy isolates the step and returns a diagnostic. This is NUREG/CR-6303's functional diversity, and it is the barrier that makes the verification beta small, because a deterministic channel shares no training data with the agent.
  • Graded oversight by consequence (designed, Section 4): the AI's influence on the decision and the decision's consequence set the oversight class, from auto-execute to two-person human review.
  • Reversible-first execution and limiting conditions for autonomy (to build): actions are staged so that the first steps can be undone; irreversible steps wait for the oversight class to clear; budgets of tokens, tool calls, time and scope per partition, with a defined response when exceeded.
  • Human role designed against the ironies of automation: the human decides, with the evidence in front of them; they do not merely confirm a recommendation.

Level 4: severe-condition management (layer 5)

Assume the agent is adversarial or badly wrong and the monitors have been fooled. The barriers here share no code path with the agent.

  • Partition isolation (to build): discipline agents (core physics, thermal-hydraulics and process, structures and materials, independent verification and licensing) run in separate partitions with no shared mutable context, each with its own time, iteration and token budget enforced by a watchdog. One partition cannot overwrite another's parameters, inject text into it, or starve it. This is ABB Atom's four divisions and ARINC 653 in software.
  • Transactional shared system model (partial; EvidenceLedger records, the transaction semantics are to build): the authoritative state of the design lives in a versioned system model (SysML v2 or equivalent), never in a prompt. A change to a shared parameter enters a pending transaction; coupled-dependency invalidation requires every consumer discipline to re-verify against the new value before the transaction commits; any failed margin rolls the state back atomically to the last verified version. This closes FM-3.1 and FM-3.2 by construction.
  • STPA unsafe-control-action filter (to build): every proposed action is checked against the four UCA types with respect to the current system state, including the rule that a dependent action cannot run before its prerequisite verification is sealed in the ledger.
  • Run-time assurance with a deterministic fallback (the core catcher) (partial; HazopAuditor is a post-hoc limit check, the Simplex switch is to build): a small, verified set of survival envelopes (peak clad temperature below the 10 CFR 50.46 limit; vessel pressure below 1.1 times design; DNBR above its limit; stress below yield over the safety factor; a dose below the label maximum; a lot not releasable without a QA record) expressed as barrier functions on the state. The monitor contains no model code. When a proposed trajectory would cross the boundary, it trips the agent offline and hands control to a pre-qualified conservative controller or to the last sealed safe state. This is the reactor protection system and the Therac-20's hardware interlocks; the lesson of the Therac-25 is that it must not be replaced by software judgement, and the lesson of Simplex is that this is the component the certification rests on.
  • Tiered FDIR (partial): Tier 1 in the harness retries bounded local faults; Tier 2 isolates a partition whose reliability has degraded (Pass^k falling below a threshold, or a coupling conflict) and switches it to a diverse model family or method, rolling the system model back; Tier 3 freezes autonomous exploration, locks the design to a verified conservative baseline, and raises a non-conformance report to the responsible engineer.
  • Control protocols chosen by threat model (designed): trusted monitoring with defer-to-trusted, audit budgets, and resampling where it helps, with the knowledge that resampling can be subverted by an adaptive attacker.
  • Control evaluations as the severe-accident analysis (built for the confessing adversary; the non-confessing one is Release 0.2): red-team policies iterated against the oversight stack, run before and after every qualified change.

Level 5: mitigation and learning (layers 5 and 6)

  • Tamper-evident record (built locally): every prompt, tool call, input, seed, solver version, reviewer decision and sign-off hashed into a write-once ledger, in the form 21 CFR Part 11 and NQA-1 ask for.
  • Incident program (partial): every unsafe approval, near miss, task defect and monitor false alarm is logged, classified by FM identifier, and analysed, in the way licensee event reports and device adverse-event reports are. The pilot's run records (changes made after seeing agent behaviour; task defects found by monitors) are the seed.
  • Corrective action (built as practice): findings change tasks, prompts, tools, thresholds and barriers, with re-qualification.
  • Operating experience sharing (to build): failure modes and fixes shared across sites and vendors, the role INPO plays.
  • Periodic re-qualification (partial): the Level 0 evidence is re-run on a schedule and after every change to the model, skills or tools, under a pre-specified change-control plan.

Independence between levels

Levels 1 and 2 use different mechanisms (declarations and contracts versus gates and review). Level 2's monitors differ from the agent in family or information. Level 3's second channel and Level 4's protection layer use no model at all, and Level 4's partitions share no mutable state. Level 5 is organisational. Independence "as far as practicable" is the design target, and the common-cause assessment in Section 4b is how it is checked.

4. The graded-oversight rule

The V&V 40 matrix, applied to an agent's decision rather than a simulation's prediction. AI influence is how much of the decision rests on the agent (sole basis; major input with other evidence; supporting; informational). Consequence is what a wrong decision does (catastrophic: loss of life or a severe release; critical: serious injury, major damage or regulatory breach; marginal: rework or delay; negligible).

4b. Quantifying the stack: the event tree and the beta factor

Nuclear practice turns the levels into numbers with an event tree: for each initiating event, the frequency with which the barriers all fail is the product of the initiating frequency and the conditional failure probabilities of the barriers in sequence. The same arithmetic applies to an AI engineering decision, and it is the honest replacement for an "alignment score".

For a class of situations m in which the correct decision is REJECT, arising with frequency f_m, an unsafe execution requires the agent to approve (barrier 1 fails), every Level 2 monitor to miss (barrier 2), the Level 3 checks and oversight class to let it through (barrier 3), and no Level 4 interlock to stop it (barrier 4):

F_unsafe = sum over m of  f_m  x  q1(m)  x  q2(m)  x  q3(m)  x  q4(m)

where each q is the probability that the barrier fails given that the ones before it have failed. The conditioning is the whole point. q2 is not the monitor's miss rate on random traffic; it is its miss rate on exactly the approvals a wrong agent produces, which is what the simulated-miss arm of Release 0.1 measures.

Common-cause failure enters through the beta-factor model used for redundant channels. If monitor A shares the agent's model family and monitor B is diverse, then

q2 = [ beta + (1 - beta) x qA ]  x  qB

with beta the fraction of the agent's failures that monitor A shares by construction. For "an LLM judging an LLM" the earlier architecture note asserts that beta approaches one on out-of-distribution transients. That is a hypothesis, not yet a measurement; what the pilot shows is that beta is not zero (Haiku as monitor shared Haiku as agent's wrong reading of N5) and not one (the Haiku monitors caught 100 percent of simulated misses written by Sonnet). Measuring beta per model pair and per hazard class is research item 4, and the design response does not wait for the number: a deterministic second channel has beta of zero with respect to any model by construction, which is why Level 3 is built from solvers rather than from a third model.

What the pilot already puts into the tree (two-sided 95 percent Wilson bounds):

Term Measured value Source
q1, honest agent, these case files 0 of 132 per model; at most 2.8 percent Release 0, Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5
q1, agent told to get unsafe cases approved Haiku 0 of 44; Opus 5 of 44 (11 percent), each disclosed Release 0.1 red-team arm
q2, grounded Sonnet monitor at a 2 percent false-alarm budget 0 of 129 misses escaped; at most 2.9 percent Release 0.1 simulated misses
q2, grounded Haiku monitor at a 2 percent budget 0 of 129 same

q2, deterministic rule checker alone 71 percent escaped (87 percent on document hazards) same
q3, q4 not measured: the pilot has no Level 3 channel or Level 4 interlock Release 1

Two readings. First, barrier order matters less than barrier independence: a grounded monitor of a different family reduces the residual by a factor of at least thirty on these cases, while the rule checker alone reduces it by less than a third, because it shares the agent's inputs. Second, the terms that dominate the residual for a catastrophic class are q3 and q4, which the pilot cannot yet measure, and the hard part of those is the beta between whatever channels remain; that is the argument for the solver-based second channel and the model-free protection layer before any further monitor work.

5. Meeting the three requirements

5.1 The AI understands the physics and chemistry (FM-1.x)

Understanding is tested, not assumed. The qualification suite distinguishes recall from understanding by using property-based ground truth: dimensional homogeneity, limiting cases, conservation, monotonicity, symmetry, and regime boundaries, where the right answer can be computed from the physics rather than looked up. Before computing, the agent declares the phenomena, regimes and models it is using (the PIRT gate), and the task includes cases where the declared model is wrong for the regime. After computing, the conservation and second-law gateways check the result independently of the agent. Learned surrogates in the loop are qualified under V&V 20/40 with a declared domain of validation and, where possible, conformal uncertainty bounds; the agent's use of them is scored by whether it respected the domain, not by whether the number looked right.

Metrics: Pass^k by hazard class (unit, regime, missing term, aggregation, envelope, fidelity, stale input); PIRT declaration accuracy against the method of record; gateway rejection rate and the fraction of rejections that were true violations; envelope-attestation accuracy.

5.2 The AI performs the task correctly (FM-2.x)

Correct performance means the right function, the right method and the right parameters, verifiably. The method registry makes the formula of record callable code, so that the agent selects rather than reconstructs it; the method-selection matrix in the skill pack maps problem class to qualified method; typed tool contracts with pre- and post-conditions refuse out-of-contract calls and unconverged results; the convergence gate makes a single-resolution answer inadmissible; the dissimilar second channel catches the wrong-input and wrong-method classes that a checker reading only the reported numbers cannot; and the format contract makes every reported check comparable. Task contracts enumerate required steps. Qualification is harness-controlled and reports Pass^k, because a buyer in a regulated industry pays for consistency, not for a good average.

Metrics: tool-selection error rate against typed contracts; method-match rate; convergence-gate failure rate and the share of "exit code 0" results it rejects; agreement rate between channels; format-contract compliance; Pass^k; catch rate of independent recomputation on simulated wrong-input misses.

5.3 The AI manages work at large scale (FM-3.x)

A plant licensing program, a drug through manufacturing, or a lunar base is a hierarchy of control: program, project, task, each level a controller with a model of the process it controls (STAMP). The design applies the same five levels at every tier and adds what scale introduces.

  • Partitioned disciplines with typed interfaces: discipline agents in isolated partitions with their own budgets, exchanging state only through producer-consumer contracts on named parameters, so that an error in one cannot travel silently and a runaway in one cannot starve another.
  • Authoritative state outside any model's context: the system model holds the constraints and the current design; agents read and propose, the ledger commits, and a constraint set at step one is still enforced at step one hundred.
  • Error-propagation analysis: cross-stage fault trees that ask what happens downstream if an upstream value is wrong, with coupled-dependency re-verification at each commit.
  • Visibility as infrastructure: agent identifiers, activity logs and real-time monitoring; "documents read" and "tools called" are first-class signals, because the pilot showed that monitors without the evidence degrade.
  • Decomposition with checkpoints: long tasks are split so that each stage ends in a verifiable, sealed state, and budgets per stage act as limiting conditions, because reliability falls with horizon and an 80 percent horizon is far shorter than a 50 percent one.
  • Common-cause control across agents: agents that share a base model, a prompt or a data source share failure modes. Diversity is required at the points where a correlated error would be catastrophic, and the FDIR tier can swap the family when a partition degrades.
  • Alarm management: the total human review load is a design quantity. Thresholds are set so that the flags a reviewer sees are worth reading.
  • Run-time assurance at the program boundary: the severe-accident barrier for an AI-run program is a model-free monitor on the survival envelopes with a trip to a conservative baseline, and the severe-accident analysis is a control evaluation with a red-team policy that does not confess, run against the whole stack.
  • Progression up the ladder only on evidence: an agent moves from single problems to projects to programs only after the residual unsafe-execution rate has been measured at the rung below under the oversight stack it will have at the rung above. This is the scale ladder in the PRD, with the oversight classes of Section 4 as the gate.

Metrics: residual unsafe-execution rate per oversight design, as a function of task horizon; monitor catch at audit budget by stage; agreement between diverse monitors on known-wrong decisions (the beta estimate); transaction rollback rate and the share of rollbacks that prevented a margin violation; trip rate of the run-time monitor and its false-trip rate; human review load and reviewer accuracy.

7. Research program

Ordered by what each result unlocks. Items 1-3 are Release 0.2 (in progress on the API credit); items 4-8 are Release 1 and build in the solver and ledger layers of the local harness.

  1. Failure-mode catalogue (3 months). Publish the FMEA of Section 2 as a living document with a case for every mode, drawn from incident reports in the three industries and from AI engineering benchmarks, with STPA unsafe-control-action analysis for the program tier. This is the artefact a regulator can review.
  2. Physics-understanding evaluations with property-based ground truth (3 months). Extend the task suite with regime, conservation and limiting-case checks computed from the physics, and harder unit and precision traps, following the error classes the physics benchmarks report; add the PIRT declaration to the task contract and score it.
  3. Harder adversaries and misses (now). Red-team policies that do not confess, iterated against a held-out monitor; agents that skip listing documents; misses that require deriving a quantity; a second writer family; calibration curves per monitor; the harness-effect arm on the SDK backend.
  4. Monitor diversity and common-cause measurement (3 months). Measure agreement between monitors of different families on known-wrong decisions; estimate beta per model pair and hazard class; test whether a deterministic recomputation channel is independent of both.
  5. Method-correctness evaluations graded by solvers, with the Level 1-3 gates built (3-6 months). Release 1 of the product plan in the local harness: a PIRT validator (pirt_validator.py), explicit conservation and second-law residual gateways in the thermo and process verifiers, a convergence gate (vv40_convergence.py) with Richardson extrapolation and Monte Carlo stationarity checks, and the dissimilar second channel with a comparator, on task families graded by OpenMC, MOOSE, CalculiX and DWSIM. Measure each gate's rejection rate and true-violation share.
  6. Graded-oversight calibration (3 months). For each oversight class, the catch rate and false-alarm cost of reaching 99 percent catch, with thresholds calibrated on real traffic; publish the table in Section 4 with measured numbers and the event tree of Section 4b with measured q's.
  7. Scale: partitions, transactions and non-confessing adversaries (6-12 months). Programs of 5-20 linked decisions across two or more disciplines, with the transactional system model (ledger transactions with two-phase commit), partition budgets, and the STPA UCA filter; red-team policies iterated against the oversight stack; human-overseer studies of review load and accuracy; horizon-stratified reliability.
  8. The run-time assurance layer (6 months, with domain partners). Upgrade the post-hoc limit check into a Simplex switch with barrier functions and a deterministic fallback; for each domain, what can be hard-interlocked, what cannot, and what the residual risk is when the interlock is the only barrier left. Trip and false-trip rates are the metrics.
  9. An operating-experience program for AI engineering decisions (ongoing). An incident taxonomy keyed to the FM identifiers and a shared database, with the corrective-action loop into tasks, prompts and barriers.

Where the evidence would plug into regulation: the NRC's AI strategic plan (NUREG-2261) anticipates licensee use of AI and prepares staff to review it; the FDA's lifecycle guidance asks for exactly the validation and performance- monitoring evidence this design produces; ISO/IEC TR 5469 and EASA's learning assurance give the structure a safety case for an AI engineering agent would follow.

7b. Claims in the existing ProximaB documents to verify before they are cited

The earlier documents are the right architecture. Some of their numbers and labels would not survive a reviewer, and they should be fixed before they are cited.

  1. The SMR evaluation report's certificate. PX-NUC-EVAL-2026-09 reports a five-task battery in which every non-baseline candidate scores Pass@1 of 100 percent, with an average latency of 0.02 ms and a token cost of $0.0014 to $0.03 per solve, and declares a "ProximaB-Qualified Agent" "certified for Grade A deployment". A latency of 0.02 ms is not a model call; those rows record scripted or cached candidates, not live frontier models, and a battery on which every candidate scores 100 percent at Pass@1 does not discriminate. The report should be re-labelled as a pipeline test of the harness, and the certificate language removed: under NQA-1 a vendor cannot certify its own agent, and "qualified against battery X, version Y, by Z" is the defensible form.
  2. "Beta approaches 1.0 for an LLM judging an LLM." Stated as fact in the architecture note; it is a hypothesis. The pilot gives the first data (Section 4b): nonzero, and well below one on these tasks. Cite it as the thing research item 4 measures.
  3. The CDF formula. The note's event tree multiplies a bracketed dual-channel term by the remaining barriers as if all were independent of the initiating condition and of one another. Section 4b restates it with explicit conditioning, which is how the pilot's measurements enter.
  4. Benchmark figures quoted from The Engineering Brain (MDGym's 84-98 percent execution against about 21 percent correctness; StructureClaw's 82.9 against 22.0 percent; the CAE study's +15.5 points; Pass@1 of 46 percent falling below 28 percent at Pass^3) are secondary citations, and some combine different benchmarks in one sentence. Quote each with its source and date, or not at all.
  5. Named thresholds (residuals of 10^-4, 10 pcm, GCI tolerances, Pass^3 over Pass@1 of 0.9 for "Grade A") are design choices. Present them as proposed acceptance criteria to be justified per domain, not as standards requirements.
  6. Standards read through secondary sources. As in v1, several standards cited here were read through summaries; clause numbers should be checked against the primary documents before any regulatory or academic use.
